A Life Dedicated to Education in Love

Our Foundress, St. Claudine ThÃ©venet, was born in Lyon, France on 30 March 1774. When the French Revolution broke out in 1789, two of Claudine's brothers were among those arrested for opposing the new regime. Claudine visited them in prison and followed the prisoners to the place of execution. As they passed by, they whispered: "Forgive Glady, as we forgive." She certainly forgave those who had denounced her brothers, but the young girl of 19 was never to forget the cruel scene of her brothers being clubbed to death after they were shot.

Claudine knew that ignorance of God, a lack of love and a sense of insecurity were the main causes of the demoralization of the people after the French Revolution. She undertook the care and education of stray girls, and other young ladies joined her. By 1818 they formed a Religious Congregation. They chose Claudine as their Superior the Foundress of the Congregation of the Religious of Jesus and Mary.

Her Pedagogy

A Pedagogy of Love

St. Claudine's pedagogy was a system of prevention that foresees and forestalls faults, giving guidance at the opportune time. It stressed attention to the individual and the dignity of each person; participation and collaboration, because love cannot develop except in a united school community; practical formation that enables the young to prepare for the future; social formation the ability to take their place in society with confidence; and a pedagogy based on simplicity and a family spirit, giving each one the taste for going beyond the self.

Her spirit lives on in our school. Every morning at St. Anthony's, we remember that the work of education in love remains the best reform we can offer for the Finest Methods Effect Nothing without Love.
